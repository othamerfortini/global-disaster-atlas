# Phase 0.3 — Real-Source Audit

Audit status: **in progress; audited tranche only**.

This document deliberately does not present the planned 100-event benchmark as complete. Values below were checked against primary/official public sources during the audit on 2026-08-28.

## Audit rules

1. A value is `verified` only when an identifiable source explicitly supports it.
2. The source URL, source organization and source definition are retained.
3. Conflicting official values are preserved as separate observations; the Atlas does not silently choose one.
4. `unknown` is never converted to zero.
5. Event-chain impacts are not summed across linked events unless the affected population is demonstrably disjoint.
6. `reported`, `estimated` and `modeled` remain distinct.
7. Economic `damage`, `loss` and `reconstruction_needs` are separate metrics.

## Verified observations

### GDA-EQ-2010-HTI — Haiti earthquake

- Event: 2010-01-12 Mw 7.0.
- USGS landslide map/report: 158,679 fatalities and more than 300,000 injuries; also maps 23,567 earthquake-triggered landslides.
- USGS publication "Overview of the 2010 Haiti earthquake": estimated 300,000 deaths and more than one million displaced.
- The two USGS publications therefore demonstrate a material casualty-definition/source discrepancy. Both observations must be retained.
- Canonical action: do not treat 158,679 and 300,000 as simultaneously additive. Store them as separate observations with definitions and publication context.

Sources:
- https://www.usgs.gov/maps/map-landslides-triggered-january-12-2010-haiti-earthquake
- https://www.usgs.gov/publications/overview-2010-haiti-earthquake

### GDA-EQ-2011-JPN — Tohoku earthquake

- USGS event identifier in the current seed: `usp000hvnu`.
- Event origin: 2011-03-11 05:46:24 UTC.
- Magnitude: Mw 9.1 in the current canonical record.
- The event triggered the 2011 Japan tsunami.
- USGS congressional record reports approximately 15,700 killed and notes the tsunami and severe nuclear-plant damage.
- The current canonical casualty values (15,901 deaths, 6,152 injured, 2,519 missing) must remain source-specific rather than being presented as a universal final count.

Sources:
- https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/usp000hvnu
- https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/lists-maps-and-statistics

### GDA-TS-2004-IO — Indian Ocean tsunami

- NOAA NCEI identifies the 2004-12-26 Mw 9.1 Sumatra earthquake at 3.316 N, 95.854 E and depth 30 km.
- NOAA reports 227,898 dead or missing in its recent/significant-event record.
- NOAA's Global Historical Tsunami Database documentation reports 227,899 killed or missing/presumed dead in its overview and approximately 1,126,900 displaced in the event summary; another NOAA poster reports 227,899 killed or missing and approximately 1.7 million displaced by the earthquake and subsequent tsunami.
- The earthquake and tsunami death toll cannot be cleanly separated because the tsunami followed within minutes.
- Canonical action: preserve the different NOAA observations and their definitions; do not invent a single precise number.

Sources:
- https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/recent-significant-events
- https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/global-historical-data
- https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/IO_Poster_20241030_final_lowres.pdf

- NOAA recognizes the 2011 Japan event as a tsunami generated by the Mw 9.1 Tohoku earthquake.
- The tsunami and earthquake form a linked disaster chain.

Sources:

### GDA-TS-1960-CHL — Chile earthquake and tsunami

- NOAA identifies the 1960 Chile earthquake as magnitude 9.5, the largest instrumentally recorded earthquake.
- NOAA states that deaths associated with the earthquake and tsunami are uncertain and estimated between 490 and 5,700.
- This is a valid example of a range rather than a single canonical count.

Source:
- https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/day-1960-chilean-earthquake-and-tsunami

### GDA-FL-2022-PAK — Pakistan floods 2022

- World Bank-led PDNA reports 33 million people affected and more than 1,730 deaths at the time of assessment.
- The same assessment reports USD 14.9 billion total damage, USD 15.2 billion total economic loss and USD 16.3 billion recovery/reconstruction needs.
- The PDNA explicitly defines damage as direct costs to damaged/destroyed physical assets, loss as changes in economic flows, and needs as recovery/reconstruction costs; these are not interchangeable.
- More than 8 million displaced people were reported in the October 2022 assessment.

Sources:
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/10/28/pakistan-damages-and-economic-losses
- https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/4a0114eb7d1cecbbbf2f65c5ce0789db-0310012022/original/Pakistan-Floods-2022-PDNA-Main-Report.pdf

## Important finding

The real-source audit already proves that the main challenge is not merely finding numbers. It is preserving **definitions, temporal reporting state, source lineage and event-chain relationships**.

For example, NOAA's 2004 tsunami records contain different displacement figures depending on the scope/definition used, while the Haiti earthquake has materially different official casualty estimates. A single flattened number would destroy useful information.

## Gate for the 100-event benchmark

An event may enter the final 100-event benchmark as `audited` only when:

- event identity is verified;
- date/location are verified;
- at least one primary or authoritative source is recorded;
- every populated critical impact value has source-level provenance;
- definitions are captured for deaths/affected/displaced where ambiguous;
- economic measures are semantically classified;
- cascade relationships are evidence-backed;
- conflicts are either resolved or explicitly left unresolved.

Until these conditions are met, the event remains `pending` and must not contribute to the final coverage percentage.
